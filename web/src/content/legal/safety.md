AgsSwap doesn't handle payment, pickups, or hauling, so a little caution goes a long way.

**Before you meet**

- Keep talking in AgsSwap messages until you're ready to meet, so there's a record if something goes wrong.
- Ask for extra photos, video of equipment running, hour-meter readings, and for animals, recent records (vaccinations, Coggins, health papers). Confirm the quantity, condition, and price before you drive out.
- **Never pay in advance** to someone you haven't met. Avoid wire transfers, gift cards, crypto, and "I'll overpay, send me the difference" offers. Those are common scams, especially with livestock, puppies, and horses that "just need shipping."
- Don't share your gate code or exact location until you're ready to arrange pickup.

**Meeting up**

- For small items, meet in a **public, well-lit place** during the day, like a feed store or sale barn parking lot, or a police station "safe exchange" spot.
- For equipment, hay, and animals that need a ranch pickup, **have someone with you**, tell a friend where you're going, and go in daylight.
- Trust your gut. If something feels off, leave.

**Inspecting**

- Look items over before you pay: hydraulics, tires, welds, hitch and safety chains, wiring, and rust-through on trailers.
- For hay, check for mold, heat, weeds, and moisture. For alfalfa, ask about blister beetles.
- For animals, watch them move, check eyes, nose, feet, and body condition, and **ask your veterinarian** before buying. Keep new animals apart from your herd until you know they're healthy.
- Make sure brands, bills of sale, and titles match the person selling.

**Paying**

- Use a payment method you trust, and **only pay once you have the item or animal**. Cash is simple. With payment apps, be sure you know who you're paying.
- Write a simple **bill of sale** for animals, trailers, and equipment.

**Loading and hauling**

- Bring the right trailer, straps, chains, and a helper. Know your truck's towing and tongue-weight limits.
- Keep clear of PTO shafts, three-point hitches, and raised loaders. Never stand between a tractor and an implement while hitching.
- Load animals calmly with proper panels or a chute, and haul them with enough space, ventilation, and water.
- In Texas heat, bring water, haul animals in the cooler part of the day, and don't overdo heavy lifting.

**If something goes wrong**

- Use **Report** on the listing or user, and email hello@agsswap.com.
- For suspected livestock or equipment theft, contact your county sheriff and the **Texas & Southwestern Cattle Raisers Association** special rangers.
- If you're in danger or a crime has happened, call **911** or your local police.
